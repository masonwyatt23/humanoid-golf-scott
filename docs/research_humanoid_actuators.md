# Humanoid actuator / joint specs vs. our golf-model needs (researched 2026-10-17)

Machine-readable twin: `data/references/humanoid_actuator_specs.json` (generated by `scripts/gen_humanoid_actuator_specs.py`; every value has source id, source type, confidence, `unverified`). Schema test: `tests/test_humanoid_actuator_specs.py`.

**Bottom line.** Only Unitree publishes usable joint numbers (spec pages + official URDF). Atlas, Optimus and Figure publish **no per-joint torque, speed or gear ratio**, so they cannot be assessed. No platform has a verified wrist near our recommended 120 N·m; the best verified wrist is Unitree H1-2 at ~19 N·m (URDF) / ~30 N·m (spec page). Waist/pelvis yaw is the one need that verified hardware meets.

Legend: **V** = official spec sheet or official model file; **M** = media/third-party; **U** = unverified / not published. Peak torques are vendor peaks, not continuous. URDF `effort`/`velocity` are control-model limits (not at-load), and sometimes disagree with spec sheets (flagged).

## 3. Platforms with no joint data
- **Atlas (electric)** official sheet (12/23/2025): 56 DoF, 1.9 m, 90 kg, payload 50 kg instant / 30 sustained / 20 one-handed, reach 2.3 m, 4 h battery (2 h heavy), 3 min self-swap, IP67, "continuous" range of motion. Joint torque/speed/gear: **not published**. Only qualitative CEO claim ("stronger at most joints than an elite athlete").
- **Tesla Optimus**: AI Day 2022 portfolio of 3 rotary + 3 linear actuators (28 total), rotary torque classes 20/110/180 N·m and linear 500/3900/8000 N, harmonic gearing — taken from media summaries, **not checked against the primary video**, and pre-Gen 2. Gen 2 (Dec 2023) official claims are relative only (−10 kg, +30% walking speed, 11-DoF hands, 2-DoF neck). V3 hand/forearm details come from patents via media and may be superseded. No wrist/hip-yaw/waist data.
- **Figure 02/03**: Figure's own releases give hand DoF (16), battery (2.25 kWh), F03 −9% mass and "2× faster actuators". Mass/height/payload numbers exist only on aggregators (payload quoted as 20, 25 and 40 kg: conflicting). One "order page" on a non-figure.ai domain was found; it is treated as untrusted.

## 4. Our model needs (from `docs/research_350yd_profile.md` / `power_profile_350yd_targets.json`)
Current model: wrist 60 N·m, damping 2, elbow 120, chest yaw 120 with ±45°, no pelvis yaw. Recommended: wrist ~120 N·m and low damping (≤0.5), ≥26.5 rad/s (130 mph club speed), pelvis yaw ±60° / ≥500°/s / ~150 N·m, chest ±70°. Note the 120 N·m is a model-derived figure (assumes damping 2); the only measured human reference in the repo is Nesbit 2005 max grip torque ~42 N·m. Derived here (D, optimistic): with damping 0.5, a 50% torque margin at 26.5 rad/s needs only τ ≥ 2·b·ω ≈ 26.5 N·m, but available torque falls with speed, so treat as a floor.

## 6. Recommendations for the Trainer/Evaluator
1. Use H1-2 as the "real hardware" envelope: wrist ≈ 19-30 N·m @ ≤31 rad/s, waist yaw 200 N·m @ 23 rad/s, ±135°. A 120 N·m wrist is 4-6× above any verified part; if kept, label results "super-human hardware" not "robot-realistic".
2. Add a waist/pelvis yaw joint with ≤200 N·m, ≤23 rad/s, ±135° (G1 variant: 88 N·m, 32 rad/s).
3. Expect club speed <130 mph under verified wrist limits unless club length/other joints compensate; run the 350 yd ladder with both "model" and "H1-2-capped" actuator profiles.
4. Do not cite Atlas/Optimus/Figure torque numbers anywhere until vendors publish them.
